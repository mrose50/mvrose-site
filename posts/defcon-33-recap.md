## Why I Attended

Three primary objectives:

1. Stay current with emerging security threats
2. Find practical inspiration for security workflows at my organization
3. Reconnect with colleagues and friends

## Favorite Talks

### Amplifying Phishing Attacks with Generative AI
*Speaker: Daniel Marques*

Examined how AI enables threat actors to automate phishing campaigns, including reconnaissance, targeted communications, and infrastructure setup. AI allows bad actors to easily customize and scale phishing operations that were previously difficult to execute manually.

### Zero Trust, Total Bust — Breaking into thousands of cloud-based VPNs with one bug
*Speakers: David Cash & Rich Warren*

Demonstrated ZTNA solution exploits allowing authentication bypass, device posture spoofing, and privilege escalation. Key lesson: vendors' Zero Trust implementations have flaws. Organizations should actively monitor logs and maintain current infrastructure.

## Villages

**Red Team Village** — Introduced new red team tools and methodologies applicable to internal exercises.

**Physical Security Village** — I volunteered here and developed knowledge of lock bypass techniques.

## Practical Takeaways

- Organizations should consider using AI for log analysis tasks
- Prompt injection attacks will increase as LLM adoption grows
- Threat actors are leveraging AI to create highly personalized, convincing phishing campaigns

## Notable Quotes

> "We don't have true artificial intelligence; we have a system skilled in pattern recognition that can provide relevant information."

> "Attackers have traditionally held the advantage in security, moving quickly and adapting swiftly. However, defenders using AI and automation are shifting this narrative."

## Final Thoughts

This was my fourth DEF CON. The experience reinforces how the security community embodies creativity, curiosity, and determination — whether focused on offense or defense.
